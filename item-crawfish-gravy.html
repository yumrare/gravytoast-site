Crawfish Gravy

A Louisiana gravy of crawfish tails in a dark, peppery roux with the trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper. Served over rice, cornbread, or fried fish in crawfish season.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionNew Orleans
Classic baseRice or cornbread
Prep timeAbout 40 minutes, mostly the roux
StorageRefrigerate sealed; the flavor deepens overnight.
Shelf life2 to 3 days refrigerated; freezes acceptably
Best usesRice plates, cornbread, fried fish, grits
SubstitutesShrimp for crawfish out of season; a lighter roux for a quicker pan
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Crawfish gravy rides crawfish season, roughly winter's end to early summer in Louisiana, when tails are cheap and plentiful. Étouffée is its close, richer cousin; the gravy is the weeknight form.

Making it

Cook a dark roux, then add the trinity and cook it down in the roux. Add stock and crawfish tails with their fat, and simmer briefly — tails toughen if they cook long. Season with cayenne and thyme.

Serving it

Over rice for supper, over cornbread for a Louisiana breakfast plate, and over fried fish on Fridays.

Swaps & pairings

Add tomatoes and it leans toward a sauce piquante. Serve it over grits and it crosses into Lowcountry territory.
