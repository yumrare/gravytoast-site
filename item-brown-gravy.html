Brown Gravy

The all-purpose gravy: a browned roux loosened with beef, chicken, or mixed stock, dark and savory. The default gravy of diners, roasts, and mashed potatoes everywhere.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionMidwest Diner Belt
Classic baseMashed potatoes and roast meats

StorageRefrigerate sealed; the most forgiving gravy to make ahead.
Shelf life4 to 5 days refrigerated; freezes well
Best usesRoasts, meatloaf, mashed potatoes, hot sandwiches, poutine-style fries
SubstitutesMushroom or onion gravy are its closest kin; stock cubes in a pinch

About it

Brown gravy is the general-purpose gravy of American cooking, the one a diner line cook makes by the quart. Its whole identity is the roux's color: brown it patiently and the gravy tastes like the roast it will cover.

Making it

Cook flour in fat — drippings, butter, or oil — until it turns the color of a copper penny. Whisk in stock gradually and simmer until glossy. A splash of soy or Worcestershire deepens color, a habit of diner kitchens.

Serving it

Over roast meats, meatloaf, and mashed potatoes; on fries it becomes a diner side. It is the gravy a gravy boat is for.

Swaps & pairings

Make it with chicken stock for a golden gravy, or beef stock for the dark diner standard. Deglaze the roasting pan into it whenever there is a pan to deglaze.
