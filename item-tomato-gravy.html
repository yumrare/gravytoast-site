Tomato Gravy

A roux-based gravy built on tomatoes, canned or fresh, with bacon fat or drippings. Tangy and brick-red, it is poured over biscuits, rice, or grits across the South.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseBiscuits, rice, or grits
Prep timeAbout 20 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; the flavor improves overnight as the tomato settles.
Shelf life3 to 4 days refrigerated; freezes well
Best usesBiscuits, grits, rice plates, fried pork chops
SubstitutesCanned tomatoes for fresh; stock for milk; butter for bacon fat
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Tomato gravy appears wherever tomatoes met the roux: Southern farmhouses used it to stretch a pan of drippings across a family, and tomato season made it a summer staple. It belongs to the same family as Lowcountry tomato gravies over grits.

Making it

Cook a light roux in bacon fat or butter, then add crushed tomatoes and their juice with a splash of milk or stock. Simmer until the raw tomato edge softens, about 10 minutes. Salt, pepper, and a pinch of sugar to taste.

Serving it

Over biscuits at breakfast, over rice or grits at supper. In tomato season it is served the day it is made, with fried meat alongside.

Swaps & pairings

Add cream at the end for a pink, mellow version. For heat, a dash of hot sauce is traditional at the table, not in the pot.
