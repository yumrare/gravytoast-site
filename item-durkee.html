About it

Durkee's spice house dates to the 1850s in New York, and its name rode spice tins into diners for a century. Its gravy mixes belong to the older American packet tradition — plain, pepper-forward, and built for counters.

Making it

Profiled, not cooked: its country-style mixes descend from the counter era, when a packet, milk, and a whisk were the whole gravy program of a small diner.

Serving it

Regional supermarket shelves and foodservice catalogs, where the brand's spice-trade history still carries it.

Swaps & pairings

Durkee is the catalog's heritage case: a spice house whose gravy line predates most of the diners it supplies.
