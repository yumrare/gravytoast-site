Diners & Regions

The diner index and the regional traditions that keep gravy alive: twelve regions and traditions, each with its gravies, its plates, and its ground. The growth engine of the catalog.

Appalachia

The mountain South is gravy country: sawmill and cornmeal gravies from its camp and farmhouse kitchens, chocolate gravy on its biscuits, and sorghum-sweet traditions in its hollows.

5 entries

The Deep South

The lowland South runs on sausage gravy, red-eye, and tomato gravy. Its farmhouse breakfasts built the canon the rest of the country now eats in diners.

13 entries

Texas

Texas plates its gravy big: cream gravy over chicken-fried steak and chili gravy over enchiladas. Tex-Mex and ranch cooking meet on the same table here.

8 entries

The Ozarks

The Missouri and Arkansas Ozarks are chocolate-gravy country, where the sweet cocoa gravy over biscuits is a family tradition with roots cooks still debate.

2 entries

Midwest Diner Belt

The diner belt of the Midwest serves brown gravy, hot beef plates, and fried mush under gravy. Its diners and cafeterias standardized the American gravy plate.

16 entries

Pennsylvania Dutch Country

Pennsylvania Dutch country cooks with thrift: mushroom gravies from the Kennett Square mushroom farms, rye toast, and pan gravies that waste nothing.

2 entries

Delaware Diners

Delaware diners are famous for gravy over toast and for scrapple plates. The small state's diner counters punch well above their weight in gravy tradition.

2 entries

New Orleans

New Orleans and Louisiana build gravy on a dark roux and the trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper. Crawfish gravy over cornbread is its breakfast signature.

2 entries

The Lowcountry

The Carolina and Georgia Lowcountry pairs its gravies with stone-ground grits: shrimp gravy at breakfast, tomato gravy over grits at supper.

4 entries

New England Diners

New England diners serve the holiday gravies year-round: turkey and giblet gravy, split-top rolls, and johnnycakes under gravy. Thanksgiving is its home ground.

5 entries

The Military Tradition

The armed forces codified gravy cooking at scale: creamed chipped beef on toast — SOS — and hamburger gravy fed generations of service members and then their families.

3 entries

Logging & Mining Camps

The logging and mining camps of the mountains stretched drippings with flour and water into sawmill and bulldog gravies. Camp cooks invented gravy under scarcity.

1 entry
