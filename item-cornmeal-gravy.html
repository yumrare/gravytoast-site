Cornmeal Gravy

An Appalachian gravy thickened with cornmeal instead of flour, giving a fine grain and a deep corn flavor. Built on bacon or sausage drippings with milk or water.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionAppalachia
Classic baseBiscuits or cornbread
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; stir as it cools to keep the meal suspended.
Shelf life2 to 3 days refrigerated; thins on reheating, whisk well
Best usesBiscuits, cornbread, fried potatoes, wilted greens
SubstitutesFine cornmeal only; coarse meal stays gritty
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Cornmeal gravy is a mountain solution to a wheat shortage: cornmeal was the grain Appalachian farms always had. It stays pourable longer than flour gravy and carries the taste of the cornmeal itself, which cooks defend fiercely.

Making it

Brown cornmeal in hot drippings, stirring, until it smells like cornbread. Add milk or water gradually and simmer several minutes so the meal softens fully. It thickens less than flour gravy and should stay pourable.

Serving it

Poured over biscuits or split cornbread at breakfast, and over fried potatoes at supper. It is thinner than flour gravy, served in a deeper ladle.

Swaps & pairings

Milk makes it rich, water makes it lean and cornier. A spoon of bacon crumbled on top is the mountain garnish. Half flour, half meal is the compromise pan.
