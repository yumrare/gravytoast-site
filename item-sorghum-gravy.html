Sorghum Gravy

A sweet breakfast sauce of sorghum syrup warmed with butter and a little water or cream, poured over biscuits and country ham. A Kentucky and Tennessee sorghum-country tradition.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionAppalachia
Classic baseBiscuits and country ham
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes
StorageRefrigerate the syrup base sealed; it keeps better than milk gravies.
Shelf lifeA week refrigerated; warm to serve
Best usesBiscuits, ham plates, cornbread, griddle cakes
SubstitutesMolasses, darker and stronger; cane syrup, lighter
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sorghum was the mountain sweetener before cheap sugar, made in horse-turned mills each fall. The gravy carries that season: dark, mineral, and only just sweet, meant to meet salt pork or ham head-on.

Making it

Warm sorghum syrup with butter and a splash of water or cream until it pours like a thin sauce; a bare pinch of salt sharpens it. Some cooks whisk it into a flour-thickened milk base. Serve warm, never boiled hard.

Serving it

Poured over split biscuits beside salty country ham, where sweet meets salt on the same fork. A taste of the fall sorghum-making season.

Swaps & pairings

Molasses makes a darker, more bitter pan. Stirred into milk gravy it becomes a sweet-savory breakfast gravy some families call syrup gravy.
