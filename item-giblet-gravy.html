Giblet Gravy

The Thanksgiving gravy: turkey drippings and stock thickened with a roux, enriched with the chopped, simmered giblets — neck meat, heart, gizzard, liver — and often chopped egg.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionNew England
Classic baseRoast turkey and dressing
Prep timeAbout 30 minutes, built during roasting
StorageRefrigerate sealed; skim the fat after chilling and stir it back or discard.
Shelf life3 to 4 days refrigerated; freezes well
Best usesRoast turkey, dressing, mashed potatoes, open-faced hot turkey sandwiches
SubstitutesChicken giblets and stock for a smaller bird
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Giblet gravy is the oldest form of the Thanksgiving gravy, from an era when nothing of the bird was wasted. The giblets simmer while the turkey roasts, so the gravy is built in parallel with the meal it crowns.

Making it

Simmer the giblets in water or stock while the turkey roasts; chop them fine. Make a roux from the roasting-pan drippings, add the giblet stock and pan juices, and simmer smooth. Fold in the chopped giblets off the boil.

Serving it

Passed at the table in a gravy boat over turkey, dressing, and mashed potatoes. It is a holiday gravy first, but diners in New England serve its kin year-round.

Swaps & pairings

Strain it smooth for a modern table, or leave the giblets coarse for the old style. Chopped hard-cooked egg is a Southern addition to the same pot.
