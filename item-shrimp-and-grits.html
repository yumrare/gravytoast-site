Shrimp Gravy over Grits

Shrimp gravy spooned over stone-ground grits: the Lowcountry's defining plate, born a fishermen's breakfast and now its most famous export.

CategoryPlates & Classics
Origin / RegionThe Lowcountry
Classic baseStone-ground grits
Prep timeAbout 45 minutes
StorageAssemble at serving; leftovers keep 2 days and lose the shrimp's snap.
Shelf lifeA serve-at-once plate; shrimp do not wait
Best usesBreakfast plates, supper, the Lowcountry signature
SubstitutesCrawfish gravy over grits, the Louisiana crossing
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Shrimp and grits began as a fisherman's breakfast — 'grits and shrimp' in Lowcountry telling — and stayed home cooking until restaurants carried it out of the region in the 1980s. The gravy is the dish; the name just lists the parts.

Making it

Cook the grits first and keep them loose. Build the shrimp gravy on bacon fat with peppers and stock, poach the shrimp in it briefly, and spoon everything over the grits at once.

Serving it

In a bowl, grits as the bed, shrimp and gravy pooled on top. Lemon and hot sauce at the table, by Lowcountry law.

Swaps & pairings

Tasso ham in the gravy is the restaurant style; bacon is the home style. Cheese grits underneath divide the region politely.
