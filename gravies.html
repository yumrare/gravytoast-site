Gravies

Every gravy, cataloged: the roux-based, the drippings-based, the regional, and the boundary cases. Gravies is the star category of GravyToast, home of the Gravy Catalog.

Sawmill Gravy

A milk gravy made in the pan with pork sausage or bacon drippings and flour, served over biscuits. Sawmill gravy is the foundational breakfast gravy of the upland South.

Gravies · Appalachia

Sausage Gravy

The national diner standard: crumbled pork breakfast sausage folded through a peppered milk gravy, served over biscuits. If a menu says biscuits and gravy, this is the gravy.

Gravies · The Deep South

Country (Cream) Gravy

A white pan gravy made from the drippings of fried chicken, steak, or chops, with flour and milk or cream, seasoned hard with black pepper. No sausage: the fried meat's own fond flavors it.

Gravies · Texas

Red-Eye Gravy

A thin, sharp gravy of country-ham drippings and black coffee, named for the circle of red fat that floats on it like an eye. It is sopped, not poured, over ham and biscuits.

Gravies · The Deep South

Chocolate Gravy

A sweet breakfast gravy of cocoa, sugar, flour, and milk, cooked like a thin pudding and ladled over hot biscuits. An Ozark and Appalachian tradition, served as a treat, not an everyday gravy.

Gravies · The Ozarks

Tomato Gravy

A roux-based gravy built on tomatoes, canned or fresh, with bacon fat or drippings. Tangy and brick-red, it is poured over biscuits, rice, or grits across the South.

Gravies · The Deep South

Cornmeal Gravy

An Appalachian gravy thickened with cornmeal instead of flour, giving a fine grain and a deep corn flavor. Built on bacon or sausage drippings with milk or water.

Gravies · Appalachia

Giblet Gravy

The Thanksgiving gravy: turkey drippings and stock thickened with a roux, enriched with the chopped, simmered giblets — neck meat, heart, gizzard, liver — and often chopped egg.

Gravies · New England

Ham Gravy

A thickened milk gravy built on baked-ham drippings and pan juices, the gravy of the holiday ham table. Creamier and milder than red-eye gravy, its coffee-sharp cousin.

Gravies · The Deep South

Brown Gravy

The all-purpose gravy: a browned roux loosened with beef, chicken, or mixed stock, dark and savory. The default gravy of diners, roasts, and mashed potatoes everywhere.

Gravies · Midwest Diner Belt

Onion Gravy

A brown gravy built on slowly caramelized onions, sweet and deep. The classic partner of sausages, liver and onions, and smothered pork chops.

Gravies · Midwest Diner Belt

Mushroom Gravy

A gravy built on browned mushrooms and their liquor, roux-thickened with stock. Earthy and dark, it serves both as a meat gravy and as the standard vegetarian stand-in.

Gravies · Pennsylvania Dutch Country

Chicken Gravy

A pale, rich gravy from roast or fried chicken drippings and stock, flour-thickened. The gravy of Sunday chicken dinners, mashed potatoes, and chicken-fried chicken.

Gravies · The Deep South

Turkey Gravy

Roast-turkey drippings and stock, flour-thickened and strained smooth. Giblet gravy's plainer sibling: the same holiday bird, without the chopped giblets.

Gravies · New England

Hamburger Gravy

Ground beef browned and folded into a peppered milk or stock gravy: a mess-hall and school-cafeteria staple. Served over toast, rice, or mashed potatoes; kin to SOS.

Gravies · The Military Tradition

Liver Gravy

The pan gravy made where liver was fried: drippings, flour, and water or milk, poured back over liver and caramelized onions. A diner and farmhouse plate of the Midwest belt.

Gravies · Midwest Diner Belt

Shrimp Gravy

A Lowcountry gravy of shrimp, bacon or ham, peppers, and a light roux, made to pour over stone-ground grits. A breakfast and supper dish of the Carolina and Georgia coast.

Gravies · The Lowcountry

Crawfish Gravy

A Louisiana gravy of crawfish tails in a dark, peppery roux with the trinity of onion, celery, and bell pepper. Served over rice, cornbread, or fried fish in crawfish season.

Gravies · New Orleans

Chili Gravy (Tex-Mex)

The defining sauce of Tex-Mex enchiladas: a smooth, brick-red gravy of stock, chili powder, and cumin, roux-thickened. Chili scholar Robb Walsh called it the mother sauce of Tex-Mex.

Gravies · Texas

Bulldog Gravy

A bare-bones camp gravy of flour, water or milk, and whatever grease the pan held, from the logging and mining camps. Sawmill gravy's leaner, harder-times relative.

Gravies · Logging & Mining Camps

Milk Gravy

The plain form of the Southern white gravy: fat, flour, and milk, whisked smooth and peppered. Every sausage, sawmill, and country gravy starts from this pan.

Gravies · The Deep South

Pepper Gravy

A cream gravy seasoned assertively with cracked black pepper, the Texas steakhouse form served over chicken-fried steak. Smooth, pale, and pepper-hot without chiles.

Gravies · Texas

Bacon Gravy

A milk gravy built purely on bacon drippings, flour whisked into the rendered fat, crumbled bacon folded back in. The breakfast-table gravy of farmhouse kitchens.

Gravies · Midwest Diner Belt

Coffee Gravy

A thin coffee gravy from the upland South: strong coffee stirred into pan drippings, sometimes lightly sweetened or thickened. Kin to red-eye gravy, and in some kitchens its synonym.

Gravies · Appalachia

Sorghum Gravy

A sweet breakfast sauce of sorghum syrup warmed with butter and a little water or cream, poured over biscuits and country ham. A Kentucky and Tennessee sorghum-country tradition.

Gravies · Appalachia

Sunday Gravy

The Italian-American Sunday sauce: tomatoes simmered for hours with braciole, meatballs, and sausage, called gravy in the Northeast's Italian-American kitchens. A sauce by technique, a gravy by name.

Gravies · Northeastern United States

Vegetarian Mushroom Gravy

A meatless mushroom gravy built on vegetable stock and mushroom liquor, roux- or cornstarch-thickened. The holiday table's vegetarian anchor, made to stand beside the turkey gravy.

Gravies · United States

Vegan Cashew Gravy

A dairy-free white gravy of blended soaked cashews, water, and nutritional yeast, seasoned with black pepper and sage. Creamy without milk or butter; the modern plant-based stand-in for sausage gravy.

Gravies · Modern United States

Gluten-Free (Cornstarch) Gravy

Any gravy thickened with a cornstarch slurry instead of a wheat roux: glossy, clear-edged, and gluten-free. The standard method swap for wheat-free tables, used on pan and stock gravies alike.

Gravies · Method, worldwide

Au Jus

Roast-beef drippings served unthickened, 'with juice.' Documented here as the boundary case: it is served where gravy is served, but with no thickener it is a jus, not a gravy.

Gravies · French technique, American steakhouses
