Sausage Gravy

The national diner standard: crumbled pork breakfast sausage folded through a peppered milk gravy, served over biscuits. If a menu says biscuits and gravy, this is the gravy.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseButtermilk biscuits
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; it sets firm when cold and loosens again on reheating.
Shelf life3 to 4 days refrigerated; reheats with a splash of milk
Best usesBiscuits and gravy plates, breakfast stacks, hash browns
SubstitutesTurkey sausage for pork; cream for part of the milk
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sausage gravy descends from the farmhouse gravies of the South and became a national standard as diners and chains spread it. Its defining rule: the sausage is not a garnish but an ingredient, crumbled through every spoonful.

Making it

Brown crumbled breakfast sausage in a skillet, leaving its fat. Stir flour into the fat, then whisk in milk to a simmer and cook until it coats a spoon. Season with black pepper and salt, tasting the sausage's own salt first.

Serving it

Spooned thick over split biscuits, the sausage riding on top. On diner menus it anchors the biscuits-and-gravy plate, usually with eggs alongside.

Swaps & pairings

Make it pepper-forward with cracked black pepper, or fold in a pinch of sage to echo the sausage's seasoning. Over Texas toast or grits it becomes a different plate entirely.
