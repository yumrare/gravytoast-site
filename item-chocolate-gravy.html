Chocolate Gravy

A sweet breakfast gravy of cocoa, sugar, flour, and milk, cooked like a thin pudding and ladled over hot biscuits. An Ozark and Appalachian tradition, served as a treat, not an everyday gravy.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Ozarks
Classic baseButtermilk biscuits
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed with wrap on the surface; it skins like a pudding.
Shelf life3 days refrigerated; reheats with a splash of milk
Best usesBiscuits at breakfast, cornbread, as a warm dessert sauce
SubstitutesDutch-process cocoa for a darker gravy; a cornstarch slurry for flour
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Chocolate gravy's roots are argued over — Appalachian hollers and Arkansas Ozarks both claim it, with an old theory tying it to Spanish trading-post cocoa. What is certain: farm families made a festival of it on weekend biscuits.

Making it

Whisk cocoa, sugar, flour, and salt dry in a saucepan. Add milk gradually and cook, whisking, until it thickens like a sauce. Finish with butter and vanilla. It should pour slowly, like warm pudding.

Serving it

Ladled generously over split, buttered biscuits while both are hot. It is a plate of its own at breakfast, rarely served over anything else.

Swaps & pairings

Make it dark and barely sweet for adults, or milk-chocolate sweet for children. A pinch of cinnamon is a common Ozark touch. Over cornbread it reads as dessert.
