Red-Eye Gravy

A thin, sharp gravy of country-ham drippings and black coffee, named for the circle of red fat that floats on it like an eye. It is sopped, not poured, over ham and biscuits.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseCountry ham and biscuits
Prep timeAbout 5 minutes, in the ham pan
StorageMake it in the pan and serve at once; it keeps poorly by design.
Shelf lifeBest fresh; 2 days refrigerated at most
Best usesSopping with biscuits, over country ham, over grits
SubstitutesWater for coffee makes plain ham gravy; a pinch of brown sugar softens it
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Red-eye is one of the oldest Southern gravies, born wherever country ham was fried and coffee was on the stove. Folklore links its name to the red eye of fat, and in some tellings to a general's fondness for coffee at breakfast.

Making it

Fry slices of country ham and remove them. Pour strong black coffee into the hot drippings and stir, scraping the pan, until the fat rises in a red circle. No flour, no thickening: it is ready in a minute.

Serving it

Served in a small bowl or poured shallow on the plate for sopping: torn biscuit and ham are dragged through it. It also wakes up a bowl of grits.

Swaps & pairings

Some cooks add a spoon of brown sugar or a splash of cola, both old Southern habits. Without coffee it becomes a simple ham pan sauce, closer to ham gravy.
