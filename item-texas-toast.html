Toasts & Bases

Texas Toast

White bread cut twice as thick as standard, about an inch, griddled in butter. The Texas steakhouse and diner base for pepper gravy and chicken-fried plates.

CategoryToasts & Bases
Origin / RegionTexas
Classic basePepper gravy, country cream gravy
Prep timeAbout 5 minutes on the griddle
StorageKeep the loaf sealed at room temperature; freeze slices for griddling from frozen.
Shelf lifeBest griddled fresh; the loaf keeps 3 to 4 days sealed
Best usesChicken-fried plates, gravy plates, garlic toast, breakfast
SubstitutesStandard white toast, doubled; a thick-cut brioche in a pinch
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Texas toast was born in Texas kitchens — the story credits a chain that ordered bread sliced too thick for the toaster and griddled it instead. The thickness is the recipe: it must be too thick for a standard slot.

Making it

Slice a soft white loaf thick, or buy it pre-sliced. Griddle both sides in butter until golden with crisp edges and a soft middle. Serve immediately; it waits for no one.

Serving it

Under chicken-fried steak and pepper gravy, beside eggs at diner breakfasts, and as garlic toast with supper plates.

Swaps & pairings

Garlic butter turns it into the steakhouse side. Under sausage gravy it makes a knife-and-fork breakfast that eats like a sandwich.
