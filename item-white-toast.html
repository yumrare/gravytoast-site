Toasts & Bases

White Toast

Plain toasted sandwich bread, the universal gravy base. Under creamed chipped beef it becomes SOS; under hamburger gravy, a cafeteria classic.

About it

White toast is the default bread of the American diner and the military mess alike: sliced sandwich bread, toasted plain. Its virtue is neutrality — it carries any gravy without arguing, and it crisps at the edges while the center soaks.

Making it

Toast sliced white bread to a firm gold — soft toast collapses under gravy. Diners butter it lightly or not at all when gravy is the point. Two slices per plate is the mess-hall measure.

Serving it

Laid flat in pairs on the plate, gravy poured over at the table or the counter. The toast softens as it sits, which is the point of the dish.

Swaps & pairings

Griddle it in bacon fat and it becomes a different, richer base. Day-old bread is traditional — toast was invented to rescue it.
