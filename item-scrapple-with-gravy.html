Scrapple with Gravy

Crisp-fried scrapple slices with gravy, the Delaware diner plate. A pork-and-cornmeal loaf, sliced and fried, served under milk or sausage gravy.

About it

Scrapple — the Pennsylvania Dutch 'panhaas' carried down the Delmarva — is pork scraps and cornmeal set in a loaf, sliced and fried. Delaware diners made it a breakfast institution, and gravy is its local escort.

Making it

Slice scrapple a quarter inch thick, dust lightly with flour, and fry in a hot skillet until both sides are deeply crisp. Serve with milk or sausage gravy poured over or alongside.

Serving it

Two or three slices per plate, gravy over, eggs alongside. Apple butter on the table is the Delaware counter's other tradition.

Swaps & pairings

Gravy over versus gravy beside divides Delaware counters; beside keeps the crust. Syrup instead of gravy is the Pennsylvania reading.
