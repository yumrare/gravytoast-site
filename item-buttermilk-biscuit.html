Buttermilk Biscuit

The Southern buttermilk biscuit: tall, tender, and tangy, built to be split and gravied. The single most important base in this catalog.

CategoryToasts & Bases
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseSausage gravy, sawmill gravy, chocolate gravy
Prep timeAbout 25 minutes, mixing to bake
StorageCool completely, seal, and keep at room temperature; freeze baked biscuits well.
Shelf lifeBest within hours; 2 days sealed, refreshed in a hot oven
Best usesBiscuits and gravy, sopping, ham plates, breakfast sandwiches
SubstitutesDrop biscuits for speed; a split-top roll outside the South
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

The buttermilk biscuit's chemistry is the point: acid buttermilk against baking soda gives a tender, tangy crumb that plain flour biscuits cannot match. Cathead biscuits — big as a cat's head — are the gravy-sized form.

Making it

Cut cold butter or lard into flour, salt, and soda; stir in buttermilk to a shaggy dough. Pat, fold once or twice, cut without twisting, and bake hot until tall and golden. Split while warm.

Serving it

Split and ladled with gravy, the top half set back on or left off by house rule. Also the sopping tool for red-eye and the plate for chocolate gravy.

Swaps & pairings

Lard makes the classic crumb, butter the richer one. Rolled thin with extra bake, biscuits become the crisp base some families prefer under gravy.
