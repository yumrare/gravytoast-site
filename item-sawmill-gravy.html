Sawmill Gravy

A milk gravy made in the pan with pork sausage or bacon drippings and flour, served over biscuits. Sawmill gravy is the foundational breakfast gravy of the upland South.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionAppalachia
Classic baseButtermilk biscuits
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; press wrap onto the surface so it does not skin over.
Shelf life3 to 4 days refrigerated; reheats once, gently
Best usesBiscuits, fried potatoes, pork chops, full breakfast plates
SubstitutesBacon drippings for sausage fat; evaporated milk for fresh milk
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sawmill gravy takes its name from the sawmill and logging camps of Appalachia, where cooks stretched pork drippings with flour and milk to feed a crew. Specks of black pepper and crumbled sausage in the pan are its signatures.

Making it

Crumble and brown sausage, or fry bacon, in a skillet and keep the drippings. Whisk flour into the fat until toasty, then add milk gradually, whisking to a simmer. It thickens as it heats; season with salt and plenty of black pepper.

Serving it

Ladle hot over split biscuits, open-face. It is the gravy of the full Southern breakfast plate, poured at the table over biscuits, eggs, and potatoes alike.

Swaps & pairings

For a leaner pan, build it on bacon drippings alone. Evaporated milk gives a richer, slightly caramel note. Serve over toast or fried mush when biscuits are absent.
