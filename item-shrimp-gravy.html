Shrimp Gravy

A Lowcountry gravy of shrimp, bacon or ham, peppers, and a light roux, made to pour over stone-ground grits. A breakfast and supper dish of the Carolina and Georgia coast.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Lowcountry
Classic baseStone-ground grits
Prep timeAbout 30 minutes
StorageBest eaten the day it is made; refrigerate leftovers promptly.
Shelf life2 days refrigerated; shrimp suffer on reheating, eat fresh
Best usesShrimp and grits, rice, biscuits, fried fish
SubstitutesAndouille for bacon; fish stock for chicken stock
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Shrimp gravy grew up in Lowcountry home kitchens where shrimp were breakfast food, caught that morning. Every coastal family guards a version; the constants are shrimp, pork fat, and patience with the roux.

Making it

Render bacon or ham, then build a light roux in the fat. Add onion, bell pepper, and stock; simmer, then poach the shrimp in the gravy for the last minutes. Lemon and parsley finish it.

Serving it

Spooned over a bowl of grits with the shrimp riding on top — the plate the world now calls shrimp and grits, in its home form.

Swaps & pairings

Make it spicy with cayenne, or rich with a splash of cream. Over rice it becomes a supper gravy; over biscuits, a coastal breakfast.
