Milk Gravy

The plain form of the Southern white gravy: fat, flour, and milk, whisked smooth and peppered. Every sausage, sawmill, and country gravy starts from this pan.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseBiscuits
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed with wrap on the surface against a skin.
Shelf life3 days refrigerated; reheats with a splash of milk
Best usesBiscuits, fried meats, potatoes, vegetables
SubstitutesHalf stock, half milk; cream for a richer pan
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Milk gravy is less a recipe than the method itself, taught by feel: fat until it shimmers, flour until it smells done, milk until it coats. Every Southern white gravy — sausage, sawmill, country — is this gravy with something added.

Making it

Melt butter or drippings, whisk in an equal measure of flour, and cook a minute. Add milk in stages, whisking, and simmer to a smooth, coating thickness. Salt and black pepper, tasted at the end.

Serving it

Over biscuits as the simplest breakfast in the South, and under fried meats as the house gravy of farmhouse suppers.

Swaps & pairings

Brown the roux a shade and it leans toward brown gravy. Add crumbled sausage and it is sausage gravy. The plain pan is the benchmark.
