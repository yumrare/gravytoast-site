Ham Gravy

A thickened milk gravy built on baked-ham drippings and pan juices, the gravy of the holiday ham table. Creamier and milder than red-eye gravy, its coffee-sharp cousin.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseBaked ham and mashed potatoes
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; a layer of fat may set on top and melts back in.
Shelf life3 days refrigerated; reheats gently
Best usesBaked ham plates, biscuits, mashed potatoes, ham leftovers
SubstitutesStock for part of the milk; a spoon of mustard stirred in at the end
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Ham gravy is the holiday ham's second course: the drippings of a baked ham are sweet, smoky, and salty enough to carry a gravy alone. Where red-eye is its thin, coffee-cut cousin, ham gravy is the roux-thickened family version.

Making it

Collect the drippings and juices from a baked or fried ham. Make a light roux in a few spoonfuls of the fat, whisk in milk and pan juices, and simmer to a pourable thickness. Taste before salting; the ham has seasoned it.

Serving it

Poured over sliced baked ham and mashed potatoes at the holiday table, and over biscuits with leftover ham the next morning.

Swaps & pairings

A spoon of brown sugar or pineapple juice from the ham's glaze echoes the bake. For a thinner sopping style, skip the roux and go the red-eye route.
