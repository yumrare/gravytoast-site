Gravies

Au Jus

Roast-beef drippings served unthickened, 'with juice.' Documented here as the boundary case: it is served where gravy is served, but with no thickener it is a jus, not a gravy.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionFrench technique, American steakhouses
Classic baseRoast beef
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes, from the roasting pan
StorageRefrigerate under its fat cap; lift the fat before reheating.
Shelf life3 to 4 days refrigerated; the fat cap keeps it
Best usesRoast beef, prime rib, French dip sandwiches
SubstitutesAdd flour or cornstarch and it crosses the line into gravy
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

The boundary matters because gravy's definition is the thickener. French kitchens are precise about it: jus is the juice as it runs, gravy is jus given body. GravyToast catalogs the whole shelf, edge included.

Making it

Collect the roasting juices, skim the fat, and reduce briefly with stock if needed. Season and serve as it runs — no flour, no starch. A splash of wine is a restaurant habit.

Serving it

In a small cup beside roast beef or prime rib for dipping, and as the bath of the French dip sandwich.

Swaps & pairings

It is the swap, in a sense: the zero-thickener form. Strengthen a weak jus with good stock rather than thickening it.
