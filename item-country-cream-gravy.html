Country (Cream) Gravy

A white pan gravy made from the drippings of fried chicken, steak, or chops, with flour and milk or cream, seasoned hard with black pepper. No sausage: the fried meat's own fond flavors it.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionTexas
Classic baseChicken-fried steak
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes, in the frying pan
StorageRefrigerate sealed; the pan-fresh version is best and rarely stored.
Shelf life2 to 3 days refrigerated; best made fresh in the pan
Best usesChicken-fried steak, fried chicken, smothered chops, mashed potatoes
SubstitutesStock for part of the milk; cream for richness

About it

Country gravy is the Texas and Southern steakhouse answer to a fried cutlet: the same seasoned flour that crusted the meat flavors the pan. Cream gravy and country gravy are two names for one method.

Making it

Fry the meat and pour off all but a few spoonfuls of drippings, keeping the browned bits. Whisk flour into the fat, then milk to a simmer until glossy. Season assertively with black pepper and salt.

Serving it

Poured over chicken-fried steak until it pools on the plate, and over fried chicken and pork chops the same way. It is made in the pan and served immediately.

Swaps & pairings

For a pepper-steakhouse style, finish with coarse cracked pepper and a splash of cream. For fried chicken, the same pan yields the same gravy; nothing else changes.
