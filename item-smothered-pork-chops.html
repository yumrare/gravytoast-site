Smothered Pork Chops

Pork chops browned, then braised in their own onion gravy until tender. The smothered plate: meat and gravy cooked into one thing.

CategoryPlates & Classics
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseRice or mashed potatoes
Prep timeAbout 1 hour, mostly braising
StorageStore in the gravy, refrigerated; the braise protects the meat.
Shelf lifeKeeps 3 days refrigerated and improves; freezes well
Best usesSupper plates, rice plates, weeknight cooking
SubstitutesSmothered steak, the beef form of the same braise
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Smothering is a technique before it is a dish: brown the meat, then braise it in its own gravy until the gravy and meat trade flavors. The pork chop version is the Deep South's weeknight monument.

Making it

Brown floured chops hard; soften onions in the drippings; add flour and stock or milk, then return the chops and braise low, covered, 30 to 45 minutes until fork-tender.

Serving it

Chops over rice or mashed potatoes with the braising gravy ladled over — the gravy is the sauce, the vegetable, and the point.

Swaps & pairings

Milk makes a cream-smothered chop; stock makes the brown classic. Tomatoes in the braise lean it toward the Lowcountry.
