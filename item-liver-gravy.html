Liver Gravy

The pan gravy made where liver was fried: drippings, flour, and water or milk, poured back over liver and caramelized onions. A diner and farmhouse plate of the Midwest belt.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionMidwest Diner Belt
Classic baseLiver and onions, mashed potatoes
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes, in the liver pan
StorageMake it in the pan and serve; it is a same-meal gravy.
Shelf life2 days refrigerated; best fresh from the pan
Best usesLiver and onions plates, mashed potatoes, smothered liver
SubstitutesOnion gravy when liver drippings are absent
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Liver gravy exists because liver was fried in farmhouse and diner pans and the drippings were too good to waste. The onions that smother the liver usually cook in the same pan and join the gravy.

Making it

Fry floured liver slices and set them aside; soften onions in the drippings. Add flour to the pan, then water or stock, and simmer to a loose gravy. Return everything to the pan for a final minute.

Serving it

Poured back over the liver and its onions, with mashed potatoes to catch the extra. A plate that has barely changed in a century of diners.

Swaps & pairings

Calf liver gives a milder pan; pork liver a stronger one. A splash of vinegar at the end is an old farmhouse brightener.
