Chili Gravy (Tex-Mex)

The defining sauce of Tex-Mex enchiladas: a smooth, brick-red gravy of stock, chili powder, and cumin, roux-thickened. Chili scholar Robb Walsh called it the mother sauce of Tex-Mex.

About it

Chili gravy is the sauce that defines classic Tex-Mex: smooth, earthy, and built on chili powder rather than fresh chiles. Robb Walsh, the Texas food historian, named it the mother sauce of Tex-Mex cooking.

Making it

Toast chili powder, cumin, and garlic in fat or a light roux, then whisk in beef or chicken stock. Simmer until it coats a spoon, dark red and smooth. It should taste of the chiles first, heat second.

Serving it

Over cheese enchiladas under a broiler's minute of heat, and over tamales and huevos. It is a sauce-gravy hybrid, always served hot and generously.

Swaps & pairings

Blend in a dried ancho for depth, or keep it to powder for the old San Antonio style. Over eggs it becomes a rancher's breakfast.
