Pork Chop with Sawmill Gravy

A fried pork chop under sawmill gravy, the Appalachian farmhouse plate. One pan makes both: the chop first, the gravy from its drippings.

CategoryPlates & Classics
Origin / RegionAppalachia
Classic baseThe chop, with biscuits or fried potatoes
Prep timeAbout 30 minutes
StorageStore chops and gravy separately; reheat together in the pan.
Shelf lifeA serve-at-once plate; chops keep 3 days
Best usesBreakfast plates, supper, farmhouse cooking
SubstitutesCountry cream gravy for the same method, lighter pan
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

The pork chop and sawmill gravy plate is mountain economy: one pig, one pan, breakfast and supper from the same cooking. The chop's drippings are darker than sausage's, and the gravy shows it.

Making it

Fry seasoned pork chops until the fond darkens; set them aside. Build the sawmill gravy in the drippings with flour and milk, then return the chops to the pan for a final minute.

Serving it

Chop center-plate, gravy over it and over the biscuits or potatoes beside it. A breakfast or supper plate without distinction.

Swaps & pairings

Bone-in chops give the darkest fond. Apple slices fried in the drippings before the gravy are a mountain garnish.
