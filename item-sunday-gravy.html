Sunday Gravy

The Italian-American Sunday sauce: tomatoes simmered for hours with braciole, meatballs, and sausage, called gravy in the Northeast's Italian-American kitchens. A sauce by technique, a gravy by name.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionNortheastern United States
Classic basePasta — rigatoni, ziti, or spaghetti
Prep time3 to 5 hours of Sunday simmering
StorageRefrigerate sealed; it is famously better the next day.
Shelf life4 to 5 days refrigerated; freezes excellently
Best usesSunday pasta, meatball sandwiches, holiday tables
SubstitutesA quick marinara is a different dish; there is no shortcut swap
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Sunday gravy's defenders and its critics argue the name itself: sauce, some insist, never gravy. The term is documented in print and in generations of Northeast kitchens, so the catalog records it as the usage it is.

Making it

Brown the meats, then simmer them for hours in tomatoes with garlic and basil until the sauce darkens and the meat surrenders. The meats come out to a platter; the gravy dresses the pasta.

Serving it

Over pasta at the Sunday table, the braised meats served as their own course. The gravy boat of the Italian-American holiday.

Swaps & pairings

Pork shoulder and beef both appear across families. The catalog notes the argument — gravy or sauce — without settling it, as its cooks never have.
