Coffee Gravy

A thin coffee gravy from the upland South: strong coffee stirred into pan drippings, sometimes lightly sweetened or thickened. Kin to red-eye gravy, and in some kitchens its synonym.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionAppalachia
Classic baseCountry ham and biscuits
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes
StorageMake it at the meal; the coffee dulls if it waits.
Shelf lifeBest fresh; 2 days refrigerated
Best usesHam plates, biscuits, pork chops, grits
SubstitutesRed-eye gravy is the ham-specific form of the same idea
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Coffee gravy belongs to the same upland habit as red-eye: coffee was the one strong flavor a camp or cabin kitchen always had. In some Southern kitchens the two names describe one pan; elsewhere coffee gravy is the sweetened, thickened version.

Making it

Deglaze meat drippings with strong black coffee, scraping the pan. For the thickened style, whisk in a small flour slurry or a spoon of sorghum and simmer a minute. Serve thin and hot.

Serving it

Sopped with biscuits beside country ham, or poured over pork chops. A small bowl of it sits on old mountain breakfast tables.

Swaps & pairings

Sweeten with sorghum for the mountain style, or leave it black and bitter for the camp style. The name covers both pans.
