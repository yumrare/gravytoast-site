Bulldog Gravy

A bare-bones camp gravy of flour, water or milk, and whatever grease the pan held, from the logging and mining camps. Sawmill gravy's leaner, harder-times relative.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionLogging & Mining Camps
Classic baseBiscuits or bread

StorageMade fresh at the meal; it was never a keeping gravy.
Shelf lifeA same-day gravy; refrigerate leftovers 2 days
Best usesBiscuits, bread, fried potatoes, camp breakfasts
SubstitutesAny pan fat; milk when the camp had a cow, water when it did not
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Bulldog gravy is named in Appalachian coal country telling — plain, stubborn fare for hard times. Where sawmill gravy had sausage and milk, bulldog gravy had whatever the pan and the flour sack could give.

Making it

Whisk flour into hot grease — lard, bacon fat, or none at all in the leanest pans — then add water or milk and boil briefly. Salt and pepper carry it. It sets thin and plain, by design.

Serving it

Over biscuits or bread at camp tables and coal-camp kitchens, where it turned a flour ration into a meal.

Swaps & pairings

With sausage drippings and milk it grows into sawmill gravy. With coffee it leans toward red-eye's family. The plain pan is the document.
