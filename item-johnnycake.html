Toasts & Bases

Johnnycake

A cornmeal griddle cake of Rhode Island tradition, thin and crisp-edged. A New England base that takes milk gravy, syrup, or gravy-and-ham plates.

CategoryToasts & Bases
Origin / RegionNew England — Rhode Island
Classic baseMilk gravy, cornmeal gravy
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes on the griddle
StorageCool and seal at room temperature; they stale quickly.
Shelf lifeBest fresh; 2 days sealed, refreshed on the griddle
Best usesBreakfast plates, gravy bases, chowder sides
SubstitutesCornbread; a cornmeal pancake off-region
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

Rhode Island johnnycakes have protected status in local argument: made with white cap flint cornmeal and served thin and lacy. The Civil War-era name 'journey cake' is one of several folk origins the state debates.

Making it

Whisk white cornmeal with salt, a little sugar by local allegiance, and boiling water or milk to a thin batter. Fry small cakes on a hot griddle until the edges lace and crisp.

Serving it

In stacks with syrup at breakfast, or singly under milk gravy as a plated base. Rhode Island serves them alongside chowder and clam cakes too.

Swaps & pairings

Thick South County style and thin Newport style divide the state. Under gravy, the thin crisp cake holds up better.
