Country Ham with Red-Eye Gravy

A thick slice of fried country ham with its red-eye gravy, biscuits on the side for sopping. The upland South's original gravy plate.

CategoryPlates & Classics
Origin / RegionThe Deep South
Classic baseButtermilk biscuits
Prep timeAbout 15 minutes
StorageCountry ham keeps wrapped in the refrigerator; gravy is made fresh.
Shelf lifeA serve-at-once plate; ham keeps a week refrigerated
Best usesBreakfast plates, holiday ham suppers
SubstitutesCoffee gravy for the same pan without the ham name
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

This is the oldest plate in the catalog and the one that teaches gravy's first lesson: red-eye is sopped, not poured. The ham is the main event; the gravy is its sharp, coffee-dark shadow.

Making it

Fry country ham slices until the edges curl; make the red-eye in the pan with black coffee. Plate the ham, set biscuits alongside, and serve the gravy in its own small bowl.

Serving it

Ham center-plate, biscuits for sopping, grits if the table wants them. The gravy bowl passes like a sauce, a spoon at a time.

Swaps & pairings

A spoon of brown sugar in the gravy softens it for modern palates; the old plates take it straight and bitter.
