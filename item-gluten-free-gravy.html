Gluten-Free (Cornstarch) Gravy

Any gravy thickened with a cornstarch slurry instead of a wheat roux: glossy, clear-edged, and gluten-free. The standard method swap for wheat-free tables, used on pan and stock gravies alike.

CategoryGravies
Origin / RegionMethod, worldwide
Classic baseAny roast or pan base
Prep timeAbout 10 minutes
StorageRefrigerate sealed; reheat gently and whisk, it sets as a soft gel.
Shelf life2 to 3 days refrigerated; cornstarch gels can weep
Best usesHoliday roasts, pan gravies, wheat-free tables
SubstitutesArrowroot or potato starch, added off the boil
Last checkedOctober 18, 2026

About it

The cornstarch method came to gravies from Chinese-American kitchens, where slurry-thickening is the house technique, and gluten-free cooking adopted it wholesale. It asks for a lighter hand: cornstarch doubles flour's thickening power.

Making it

Simmer the drippings or stock first. Stir cornstarch with cold water — about half the volume flour would take — and whisk the slurry in at a boil. Cook one minute only; longer boiling thins it back out.

Serving it

Anywhere a roux gravy goes. Its gloss reads slightly different on the plate — clearer, shinier — which some cooks prefer for holiday birds.

Swaps & pairings

Rice flour makes a roux-style gluten-free gravy with more body. Arrowroot gives the clearest gloss but must never boil hard.
